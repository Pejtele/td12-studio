TD-12 Settings Manual

Every trigger setting in the TD-12 Trigger Editor: what it does, what it can affect, and examples. Independent project, not affiliated with Roland.

Where settings live

  • Four trigger banks. Each bank holds its own settings for all inputs, so you can keep one bank per drum set or room.
  • Only the active bank affects your playing. The active bank is chosen on the module (TRIGGER → F1 BANK). Editing another bank is safe for experiments, but you won't hear a difference until it's selected. The editor can't show which bank is active yet.
  • Software drums: if you play a drum library over MIDI, these settings reach it through which notes get sent and how hard they are. The TD-12's own volume and sound settings aren't part of this.

Trig Type

PD125 … RT3T · per input · module: TRIGGER → F1 BANK

Tells the module which pad or trigger is plugged into that input. Each type is a package of settings suited to that model, so this is always the first thing to get right.

What it can affect

  • Every setting on this page except Xtalk Cancel, because choosing a type loads that pad's defaults and overwrites your adjustments on that input.
  • Internal settings you can't see, which is why setting the correct type matters even if the visible numbers look fine.

Examples

  • A PD-105 snare set to PD100: dynamics and rim response may feel wrong even though the pad is healthy. Set it to PD105.
  • A PD-85: use PD80R, which covers PD-85, PD-80R and PD-80. A KD-85: use KD80.
  • A non-Roland pad: start with PAD1 or PAD2, then adjust by feel.
  • An acoustic drum trigger: RT7K, RT5S or RT3T, for the RT-7K, RT-5S and RT-3T.

Tip: note your custom values before changing type, or keep a backup.

Sensitivity

1–32 · per input · module: TRIGGER → F2 BASIC

How much output you get for a given hit. Higher means a soft hit can already be loud. Lower means even a hard hit stays quieter.

What it can affect

  • Overall loudness of the pad, and how hard you must play to reach full level.
  • Ghost notes: too low and they get lost.
  • Dynamic range: too high and normal and hard hits all reach maximum and sound the same.

Examples

  • Normal and hard snare hits all sound identical (MIDI velocity stuck at 127): lower it.
  • You have to hit very hard to get a strong sound: raise it.
  • Soft ghost notes disappear: raise it, and check Threshold isn't blocking them.

Tip: change 1–2 steps at a time. Play your softest ghost note and your hardest accent, and aim to keep both clearly different.

Threshold

0–31 · per input · module: TRIGGER → F2 BASIC

The minimum hit strength that counts. Anything below it makes no sound at all.

What it can affect

  • False triggers from vibrations, for example from the stand or neighbouring pads.
  • The softest notes you can play: set too high, they vanish.

Examples

  • A tom sounds faintly when you play the kick: raise that tom's Threshold step by step until it stops.
  • Very quiet ghost notes or soft ride taps go missing: lower it.

Tip: Threshold is a gate, not a volume control. Notes above it keep their normal level, and notes below it are simply gone.

Curve

LINEAR, EXP1, EXP2, LOG1, LOG2, SPLINE, LOUD1, LOUD2 · per input · module: TRIGGER → F2 BASIC

How hit strength turns into volume. The same hit can come out louder or softer depending on the curve.

Rough shapes for orientation, not exact graphs. Left to right is hit strength; bottom to top is volume.

  • LINEAR: the standard and most natural response.
  • EXP1, EXP2: most of the change happens in harder playing. Soft notes stay soft.
  • LOG1, LOG2: most of the change happens in softer playing. Medium hits get loud quickly.
  • SPLINE: extreme changes in response to dynamics.
  • LOUD1, LOUD2: very little dynamic response, so levels stay strong. Can help keep drum triggers stable.

Examples

  • You want accents to really jump out from the backbeat: try EXP1.
  • Soft playing feels too quiet and flat: try LOG1.
  • A kick trigger should hit the same on every note: try LOUD1.

Tip: set Sensitivity first and change the curve afterwards. If your drum software or DAW also has a velocity curve, change only one of them at a time.

Scan Time

0–4.0 ms · per input · module: TRIGGER → F1 BANK → F5 ADVANCE → F1 SCAN

How long the module looks at the signal before deciding how hard the hit was. Pads and triggers differ in how fast their signal rises, so too short a time can misjudge the hit.

What it can affect

  • Consistency: identical hits coming out at different volumes.
  • Delay: a longer time means the sound starts later.

Examples

  • Hits of the same strength jump between loud and quiet: raise it.
  • Roland's method: hit steadily at one strength, raise the value from 0 until the volume settles at its loudest, then check soft and hard hits still respond properly. Use the lowest value that works.
  • Going from 2.0 to 4.0 ms adds up to 2 ms of delay, about the same as moving 70 cm further from a speaker.

Retrig Cancel

1–16 · per input · module: TRIGGER → F1 BANK → F5 ADVANCE → F1 SCAN

Filters out false extra hits caused by the fading end of the trigger signal. Especially important with acoustic drum triggers, whose signals can be irregular.

What it can affect

  • Double notes from a single hit.
  • Fast playing: too high and notes in rolls can be dropped.

Examples

  • One tom hit sounds twice: while hitting repeatedly, raise it until the extra note stops.
  • Buzz or press rolls lose notes after you raised it: lower it again and try Mask Time instead.

Tip: use the lowest value that stops the double notes.

Mask Time

0–64 ms · per input · module: TRIGGER → F1 BANK → F5 ADVANCE → F1 SCAN

After a hit, the module ignores that pad completely for this many milliseconds.

What it can affect

  • Double triggers from a beater bouncing back or staying on the head.
  • Your top speed: any real note inside the window is lost.

Examples

  • The kick plays twice on one stroke: let the beater bounce against the head, and raise Mask Time until the extra sound stops.
  • Speed check: at 240 BPM, sixteenth notes on one pedal are 62.5 ms apart (60000 ÷ BPM ÷ 4). A Mask Time of 64 ms would drop them; 10 ms is safe.
  • A very tight flam or drag on one pad disappears: lower it.

Tip: keep it as low as you can. If one hit produces two or more sounds, Roland suggests adjusting Retrig Cancel.

Rim Gain

0–3.2 · per input · module: TRIGGER → F1 BANK → F5 ADVANCE → F2 RIM
For PD-125/120/105/85/80R, PD-9/8/7, CY series, VH-12/11 and RT-5S

The same idea as Sensitivity, but for the rim or cymbal edge. Higher makes it loud even when played softly; lower keeps it quieter even when played hard.

What it can affect

  • Rim shot and rim click volume on snare and toms.
  • Edge volume on cymbals, relative to the bow.

Examples

  • Crash edge hits are much louder than bow hits: lower it.
  • Rim shots are weak compared with head hits: raise it.

RimShot Adjust

0–8.0 · per input · module: TRIGGER → F1 BANK → F5 ADVANCE → F2 RIM
For PD-125/120/105/85/80R and RT-5S

How readily the module detects a hit as a rim shot.

What it can affect

  • Rim sounds appearing when you only meant to hit the head.
  • How easy real rim shots are to play.

Examples

  • A hard head hit sometimes plays a rim sound: lower it.
  • Real rim shots become hard to get after lowering it: you went too far, so raise it a little.

XStick Threshold

0–127 · one value in the editor, not per input · module: TRIGGER → F1 BANK → F5 ADVANCE → F2 RIM
For PD-125/120/105/85/80R and RT-5S

The crossover between a cross stick and a rim shot: softer rim hits below the point play the cross stick sound, harder ones the rim shot sound.

What it can affect

  • How easily you get cross sticks.
  • Open rim shots accidentally playing a cross stick.

Examples

  • Cross sticks often come out as rim shots: raise it.
  • Rim shots sometimes play a cross stick as well: lower it.
  • You never want cross sticks: at 0, a cross stick plays the open rim shot sound.

Tip: cross stick also has to be enabled in the drum kit on the module, and a software drum library has its own rim and cross stick mapping. Check those if nothing changes.

Xtalk Cancel

OFF, then steps of 5 · per input · module: TRIGGER → F1 BANK → F4 XTALK → F1
The editor allows up to 80; the module's upper limit isn't confirmed yet

Stops a pad from sounding when vibration from another pad reaches it (crosstalk). Set it on the pad that sounds by mistake, not on the pad you actually hit. It works between pads in the same Xtalk Group, which is set on the module (F4 XTALK → F3 GROUP).

What it can affect

  • Ghost sounds from pads sharing a stand.
  • Simultaneous hits: too high and the softer of two pads hit together may not sound.

Examples

  • Hitting the snare also sounds tom 1: put both in the same group and raise Xtalk Cancel on tom 1.
  • A soft tom note goes missing when played together with a loud snare hit: lower it on the tom.
  • Crosstalk won't go away: try moving the pads further apart.

Tip: use the lowest value that works. Changing Trig Type doesn't reset this setting.

A tuning order

A suggested order for this project, working from settings that change the most to the least.

  1. Trig Type, because it resets most of the others.
  2. Sensitivity, using your softest and hardest hits.
  3. Threshold, just high enough to stop false triggers.
  4. Curve, to shape the feel.
  5. Retrig Cancel and Mask Time, only if you get double notes.
  6. Rim Gain, RimShot Adjust and XStick Threshold on rimmed pads.
  7. Xtalk Cancel, with the whole kit set up on its stand.

Change one setting at a time, and play between changes. If a pad gets worse, the backup restores it.

Not in the editor yet

  • Xtalk Group and Mount Type (PAD or CYMBAL), under F4 XTALK.
  • Hi-hat settings, under F3 HI-HAT: controller type, offset, foot splash, noise cancel, CC settings.
  • 3-way trigger for a CY-15R or CY-12R/C ride, under F5 ADVANCE → F3.
